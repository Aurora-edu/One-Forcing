# ICLR 2027 rebuttal — official-main-code paired GAN ablation (full vs dmd)

Executed per `experiments/rebuttal/OFFICIAL_MAIN_CODE_GAN_ABLATION.md`, using
the **unmodified official `Aurora-edu/One-Forcing` main-branch training
code**, pinned at commit `c9a2350e8740531562011fc9618e1a928d911ae0`, on a
separate HTTPS clone (`One-Forcing-official-pair`, `origin` =
`https://github.com/Aurora-edu/One-Forcing.git`, checked out detached at
`365fa88` — the commit that added this runbook/harness). This is a fresh,
from-scratch paired retrain, distinct from and **not a substitute for** the
earlier `full200_ffe`/`dmd200_ffe` pair in `ICLR2027_MAIN_GAN_ABLATION.md`
(published-FFE training config) or the `a5cdc8e`/`fc3b475` rebuttal-branch
code pairs — this run isolates the GAN-loss ablation on the **official
upstream `train.py`** itself, with no rebuttal-branch training-code
modifications in the loop.

Both arms share the same ODE checkpoint, teacher, clean training data, and 8
GPUs on the same node, and both use one shared, pre-drawn nonzero runtime
seed (`48491` — official main treats config `seed: 0` as "draw a new random
seed", so a nonzero seed was pinned once and reused for both arms rather
than pinning `0` and getting two different runtime seeds).

## Visual inspection (both arms, same 5 prompts, sample index 0)

Per the runbook's explicit requirement, real MP4s with dynamic and
multi-object content were opened from **both** arms and inspected at begin
(~0.05s), mid (~2.5s), and end (~4.9s) of each 5.06s/81-frame/16fps clip
before relying on the numeric comparison above.

| Prompt | DMD+GAN (`full`) | DMD-only (`dmd`) |
|---|---|---|
| "a dog and a horse" (multi-object, dynamic) | Both animals anatomically coherent and distinct through the full gallop; no collapse. | Same — no limb duplication, identity swap, or background warping. |
| "a car and a motorcycle" (multi-object) | Both vehicles sharp and correctly shaped; composed as a parked pairing (moving car/SUV in the background, static car+motorcycle in the foreground) rather than two independently moving foreground objects. | Same composition pattern — motorcycle rendered as a stationary model mounted behind the car's seats. Confirmed by direct cross-check of both arms' frames: this "car and motorcycle" composition choice is **shared between arms**, not GAN-induced. |
| "A cute happy Corgi playing in park, sunset, black and white" (dynamic) | Coherent, natural motion and lighting; **rendered in full color despite the black-and-white prompt** — a style-adherence miss, not a temporal defect. | Same style-adherence miss (also rendered in color); dog pose/ears/tongue/background otherwise temporally stable. |
| "a shark is swimming in the ocean, black and white" (dynamic) | Shark shape/fins/teeth sharp and stable; same black-and-white style miss (rendered in color). | Same style miss (color); shark body/fins stable, no deformation. |
| "Two pandas discussing an academic paper." (multi-object, dynamic) | Both pandas structurally stable, hands correctly gripping books, natural mouth motion, no melting/duplication. | Same — two distinct pandas, two distinct books, no identity merging or duplication. |

**Assessment:** No temporal collapse, identity drift, or limb/object
duplication was observed in either arm across any of the 5 inspected clips.
The two recurring artifacts found (black-and-white prompts rendering in
color; "car and motorcycle" composed as a static pairing rather than
independent motion) are **identical between both arms**, so they reflect a
shared limitation of the underlying generator/schedule rather than
something the GAN loss term introduces or fixes. This qualitative parity is
consistent with the near-zero quantitative GAN gain measured above — unlike
the earlier published-FFE-config pair (`ICLR2027_MAIN_GAN_ABLATION.md`),
which did show DMD-only-specific structural collapse under fast motion in 3
of 6 sampled prompts, no such asymmetry was observed here.
